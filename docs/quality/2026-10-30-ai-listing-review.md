# AI listing review

Rental, second-hand and food listings enter a PostgreSQL queue when new or edited. Existing listings are excluded until an administrator starts a historical scan. Content hashes avoid repeating unchanged checks; new content has priority over historical jobs.

Defaults: three listings per batch, one request at a time, 60 seconds between requests, ten minutes between batches, 30 requests and 200,000 reserved tokens per UTC day. Administrator settings do not reset counters or provider cooldowns. A transaction-level advisory lock coordinates reservations across replicas; committed leases survive restarts. HTTP requests run outside database transactions and have a 30-second timeout. Expired leases can be retried, with three attempts before human review.

429 responses pause the shared moderation schedule. Retry-After and provider retryDelay are respected; daily-quota errors pause for 24 hours. There is no immediate retry. These controls reduce pressure but cannot guarantee absence of 429 responses: other application features share the provider project quota. Reserved UTF-8 input bytes plus the 512-token output cap are a conservative local allowance, not provider billing telemetry.

Only public listing text is submitted: title, description, price and category-specific fields. No photos, contact information, private messages, addresses or financial records are submitted. AI does not verify sellers, food quality or physical safety. Instructions embedded in listing text are treated as untrusted data.

Automatic hiding requires an allowed severe category, confidence at least 0.98, exact quoted evidence, and a conservative direct phrase match without nearby negation. Suspected fraud and incorrect categories go to human review. Rental posts use rejection; marketplace posts use archival only when no stock is reserved. Orders, balances and ledger records are untouched. Administrators can inspect checked descriptions and evidence, keep or hide uncertain listings, and restore automatically hidden listings. Content/version checks prevent stale results or restoration from overriding subsequent edits. Decisions retain the administrator and timestamp.
